How the EMI is worked out

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. In the early years most of each EMI is interest; the principal share grows over time.

Example

A ₹30 lakh home loan at 8.5% for 20 years has an EMI of about ₹26,035 and total interest of about ₹32.5 lakh, more than the loan itself. A single ₹2 lakh prepayment in year 3 cuts the interest by several lakh and ends the loan years earlier.

When to prepay

  • Prepay the highest-rate loan first (personal loan, then car, then home).
  • Keep your emergency fund intact before prepaying.
  • Ask your lender whether to reduce tenure (saves more interest) or EMI.

Questions

Is a prepayment charged?

For floating-rate home loans taken by individuals, banks in India generally cannot charge a prepayment penalty. Fixed-rate and other loans may have charges; check your loan agreement.

Should I reduce EMI or tenure after prepaying?

Reducing tenure usually saves more interest. Reducing EMI gives more monthly breathing room.

Does this include processing fees or insurance?

No. It shows principal and interest only.
